## What Batch Anomaly Detection does

Find production batches that look different from other batches so a person can decide what deserves investigation.

### A simple example

A batch is a group of items made together. If most batches have similar temperature, pressure, and speed patterns, a batch with a different combination may deserve attention even when no single measurement is obviously wrong.

### What you can do in this free demo

Use the built-in data, choose the part and grouping fields, and select numeric measurements. Run the analysis, review flagged batches, compare their measurements with typical batches, and explore the categorical comparisons and available downloads.

The data is synthetic and already included. You can explore the existing filters, settings, charts, and downloads without supplying company files. Changes affect your demonstration session, not production equipment or records.

### How to understand the results

The Isolation Forest model assigns unusualness scores based on numeric summaries of each batch. A flagged batch is unusual relative to this dataset. The expected unusual fraction influences how many batches are flagged. Comparisons by operator, machine, or shift help you ask follow-up questions.

### What the results cannot tell you

This app detects unusual existing batches; despite the original demo URL mentioning defect prediction, its score is not a calibrated probability of future defects. Unusual can mean a problem, a new product, or an acceptable process change. A statistical association does not prove that a machine or operator caused the difference. Use at least three meaningful numeric features.

### What a version customized for you would need

Batch identifiers, numeric process or quality measurements, part identifiers, and optional machine, shift, or operator fields. Confirmed defect labels would help evaluate whether unusualness is useful for your actual quality objectives.
